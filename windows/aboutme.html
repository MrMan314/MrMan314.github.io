About Me

From a young age, I have been interested in coding and anything computer related.

I have mostly been a self-taught learner in many areas, such as programming, software applications, hardware and networking knowledge. When I first told my parents how interested I was in programming, they found a website called [Sololearn](https://www.sololearn.com/). This was the beginning of my self-taught programming journey, though most of my knowledge, especially in the electronics domain, has come from a collective contribution of google searches and (more recently,) asking AI. In the past few years, I have also expanded my horizons into the world of RF.

Some of my recent projects and Activities include building and designing antennas for different applications, such as the GOES ground station pictured above, and attempts to reverse-engineer different devices to take full advantage of their hardware, such as a dollar store FM transmitter I modified to be able to transmit on 76 ~ 123 MHz, as opposed to just four factory-set frequencies.

In the past, I have also ran a Linux server with email services (Postfix and Roundcube webmail, later migrated to AWS SES); an Apache httpd server linking multiple web services through a reverse proxy; and a QEMU/KVM virtualization service, which required modifying the Linux kernel to allow for PCIe Passthrough on the supposedly "unsupported" machine.

I have also been interested in Cybersecurity and led a team with friends in many Capture the Flag (CTF) competitions, such as picoCTF, hosted by Carnegie Mellon University. I found web exploitation problems interesting at first, but I later moved on to PWN (binary exploitation) and REV (reverse engineering) problems, as they aligned more with my interests. In picoCTF 2025, my team placed in the top 1% worldwide among over 10000 participating entities. Outside of competitions, I found many vulnerabilities in some apps I use, as well as my grade school district's systems, and promptly reported them to the necessary individuals.
